Research subject: Francis Scott Key, born 1779 at Terra Rubra, Frederick (now Carroll) County, Maryland · Prepared by Old Line Heritage Co., November 2026

What we found

Francis Scott Key's family had been in Maryland for three generations before he was born, going back to a London-born ancestor who arrived about 1720.

  • His great-grandfather Philip Key was born in St. Paul's Parish, Covent Garden, London, on March 21, 1696/97. He came to Maryland about 1720 and served in the colonial legislature off and on from 1728 to 1764 [2][4].
  • The farm where Key was born, Terra Rubra, stayed in the family: Philip Key's 1764 will left part of it to his son Francis, Key's grandfather [4].
  • Key's grandmother, Ann Arnold Ross (1727–1811), was the daughter of John Ross of Annapolis. Her sister married the Annapolis doctor Upton Scott, whose house young Francis probably stayed in while at school [2][5].
  • Key's father, John Ross Key, joined a Maryland rifle company in June 1775 and later served as a Frederick County justice and a state legislator [2][3].

We identified 11 of the 15 people on the tree. The four still missing are all on his mother's Charlton side.

In June 1775 he was commissioned a second lieutenant in Captain Thomas Price's Maryland Rifle Company, one of the first units from outside New England to join Washington at the siege of Boston. By 1781 he was a captain commanding a Frederick County cavalry company in the Yorktown campaign [3]. At home he served as a Frederick County justice from 1777, sat in the state legislature in 1778–1779 and 1791–1792, and was a militia brigadier general from 1801 to 1808 [2].

He built “Bushwood Lodge” in St. Mary's County around 1730 and sat in the colonial Lower House in several terms between 1728 and 1754, then on the Council in 1763–1764 [2]. He married first Susannah Gardiner and, by 1747, Theodosia Lawrence, widow of Rev. John Humphreys [2]. His will, written March 10, 1764 and proved September 1, 1764, divides land in St. Mary's, Charles and Frederick counties among his children and grandchildren and asks that his late wife's coffin be placed on his [4]. It also names the enslaved people he held, records that matter to their descendants' own family research.

Still open, and where to look next

These are the questions the sources haven't settled, and where we'd search next.

  • Where John Ross Key was born. The Maryland legislature dictionary says Annapolis [2]; other accounts say Redland in Frederick County [3]. Next step: Anne Arundel and Frederick County parish registers for 1754.
  • The exact day John Ross Key died. October 12, 1821 [2] or October 11 [3]. Next step: his Frederick County probate file and newspaper death notices from October 1821.
  • Where John Ross Key was first buried. Mount Olivet Cemetery in Frederick was founded in the 1850s, so his grave there is probably a reburial. Next step: Mount Olivet's interment records and Frederick church burial registers.
  • Arthur Charlton's and Eleanor Harrison's parents (tree boxes 12 to 15). Online family trees suggest names, but none we found cites a record. Next step: Frederick County land, probate and church records for Arthur Charlton, who died in 1771.
